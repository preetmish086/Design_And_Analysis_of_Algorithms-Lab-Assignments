ALGORITHM-
Create a table:
DP[e][f]

where:
DP[e][f] = minimum number of drops required in the worst case using e eggs and f floors.

For every combination of e and f, try every possible first floor k.

for e = 2 ... E
    for f = 2 ... F
        DP[e][f] = infinity

        for k = 1 ... f
            breaks    = DP[e-1][k-1]
            survives  = DP[e][f-k]

            worst = 1 + max(breaks, survives)

            DP[e][f] = min(DP[e][f], worst)


PSEUDOCODE-
Algorithm EggDrop(E, F)

    for e = 0 to E
        DP[e][0] ← 0

    for f = 1 to F
        DP[1][f] ← f

    for e = 2 to E
        for f = 1 to F

            DP[e][f] ← ∞

            for k = 1 to f

                breaks ← DP[e-1][k-1]
                survives ← DP[e][f-k]

                worst ← 1 + max(breaks, survives)

                DP[e][f] ← min(DP[e][f], worst)

    return DP[E][F]


COMPLEXITY-
There are:

E choices of eggs
F choices of floors
F possible dropping floors for each state

Therefore:
O(EF^2)

Space:
O(EF)


note for self-
max(breaks, survives)

because we're interested in the worst case, and:

min(...)

because we choose the floor that gives the best worst-case result.
